    What is team capacity?

    Team capacity is the amount of project work your team can realistically do in a week. It is lower than headcount times contract hours, because meetings, admin, support work and absence take time away from projects.

    How this calculator works

    • Net capacity per week = people × hours × (1 − time not on projects) × (1 − absence)
    • Demand per week = the sum of each project's remaining hours divided by its weeks to deadline
    • Saturation = demand ÷ net capacity
    • Delay: for each deadline, the work that must be done by that date, assuming each project is spread evenly, minus what the team can deliver. The excess divided by weekly capacity gives the delay in weeks.

    The traffic light uses rules of thumb, not an industry standard. Up to 85% leaves room for surprises. Between 85% and 100% the plan fits but has no slack. Above 100% something has to give, and above 120% the plan is not feasible as it stands.

    Example

    Four people at 40 hours, 20% of time off projects and 10% absence give 4 × 40 × 0.8 × 0.9 = 115 hours per week. Three projects need 50 hours a week each, so demand is 150 hours: saturation is 130%. The team is short by about 1.2 people, and the first deadline slips by more than a week.

    Questions

    What saturation is healthy?

    Most teams need some slack for urgent requests, sick days and estimate errors. As a rule of thumb, keep planned load at or below 85% and treat anything above 100% as a decision to make, not a plan to follow.

    My saturation is above 100% for weeks. Is the number wrong?

    Maybe. Check that leave is included, that hours are not double counted across projects and that estimates are remaining hours, not total hours.

    Does this replace resource planning software?

    No. It is a quick check to decide whether a plan is realistic before you commit. It does not model individual skills, dependencies or day by day scheduling.
